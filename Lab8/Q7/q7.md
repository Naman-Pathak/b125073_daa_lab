# Rod Cutting with Reconstruction

## 1. Problem Statement

Given a rod of length `n` inches and an array of prices:

```text
P = [p1, p2, ..., pn]
```

where `pi` represents the market price of a rod piece of length `i` inches, determine:

1. The **maximum revenue** obtainable by cutting the rod into pieces and selling them.
2. The **exact lengths of the pieces** that form the optimal decomposition.

Cuts are integral, meaning that all piece lengths must be positive integers.

The rod may also be **left uncut** if that gives the maximum revenue.

The sum of the lengths of all pieces must be exactly `n`.

The objective is to design a **Dynamic Programming** algorithm, implement it in C, reconstruct the optimal solution, validate the algorithm using suitable input, and derive its complexity.

---

# 2. Example

Consider a rod of length:

```text
n = 8
```

with prices:

| Length |  1 |  2 |  3 |  4 |  5 |  6 |  7 |  8 |
| ------ | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: |
| Price  |  1 |  5 |  8 |  9 | 10 | 17 | 17 | 20 |

We need to determine the best way to divide the rod.

Some possibilities are:

```text
8             → 20
1 + 7         → 1 + 17 = 18
2 + 6         → 5 + 17 = 22
3 + 5         → 8 + 10 = 18
4 + 4         → 9 + 9 = 18
2 + 2 + 4     → 5 + 5 + 9 = 19
2 + 3 + 3     → 5 + 8 + 8 = 21
```

The best solution is:

```text
2 + 6
```

with revenue:

```text
5 + 17 = 22
```

Therefore:

```text
Maximum Revenue = 22
Optimal Pieces = {2, 6}
```

---

# 3. Why Dynamic Programming?

A simple recursive solution would try every possible way to cut the rod.

For a rod of length `n`, we can make the first cut at:

```text
1, 2, 3, ..., n
```

After making the first cut, the remaining rod is again a smaller rod-cutting problem.

For example:

```text
Rod of length 8
        |
   First cut
        |
   +----+----+----+
   1    2    3   ... 8
```

This creates many overlapping subproblems.

For example, the optimal revenue for a rod of length `5` may be required while solving the problems for lengths `6`, `7`, and `8`.

Instead of solving the same subproblem repeatedly, Dynamic Programming stores the result of every smaller rod length.

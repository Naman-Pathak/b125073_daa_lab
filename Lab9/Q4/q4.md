Minimum Cost to Connect Sticks
1. Problem Statement
Given n sticks with lengths L1, L2, ..., Ln, connect all sticks into one stick.
- Connecting two sticks of lengths x and y costs x + y.
- The resulting stick has length x + y.
- Find the minimum total cost required to connect all sticks.
2. Approach: Greedy Algorithm with a Min-Heap
The key observation is to always connect the two shortest available sticks first.
Why? A stick created early may be involved in several later connections, so its length can contribute to the total cost multiple times. Combining the smallest sticks first helps minimize this repeated contribution.
A min-heap is a suitable data structure because it lets us efficiently retrieve and remove the smallest element.
Algorithm
1. Read the number of sticks, n, and their lengths.
2. Insert every length into a min-heap.
3. Initialize totalCost = 0.
4. While the heap contains more than one stick:
   - Remove the two smallest lengths, x and y.
   - Calculate cost = x + y.
   - Add cost to totalCost.
   - Insert the combined stick of length cost back into the heap.
5. When one stick remains, output totalCost.
3. Example Walkthrough
Input:
4
2 3 4 6
Step	Two sticks connected	Connection cost	Remaining sticks
1	2 + 3	5	4, 5, 6
2	4 + 5	9	6, 9
3	6 + 9	15	15


Total cost:
5 + 9 + 15 = 29
Output:
Minimum total cost = 29
4. Correctness Intuition
The greedy choice is to combine the two shortest available sticks first. This is optimal because a stick created during a connection may contribute to the cost again in later connections. Keeping the lengths chosen for early combinations small minimizes their repeated contribution.
This is the same greedy principle used in Huffman coding: repeatedly combine the two smallest weights.
5. Complexity Analysis
Let n be the number of sticks.
Time Complexity
- Inserting all n sticks one by one into the heap takes O(n log n).
- Each connection performs two minimum extractions and one insertion, each taking O(log n).
- Exactly n - 1 connections are needed.
Therefore, the overall time complexity is:
O(n log n)
Auxiliary Space Complexity
The min-heap stores at most n stick lengths.
O(n) auxiliary space
6. Data Representation
The stick lengths are stored in a min-heap implemented using an array.
For an element at index i (zero-based):
- Parent index: (i - 1) / 2
- Left child index: 2 * i + 1
- Right child index: 2 * i + 2
The min-heap property ensures that the smallest stick is always at the root.
7. Implementation Notes
- The C program uses long long for stick lengths and the total cost.
- It dynamically allocates memory for the heap and releases it with free().
- Stick lengths must be non-negative integers.
- As with any fixed-width integer implementation, inputs must be small enough that combined lengths and the accumulated total do not exceed the range of long long.
8. Conclusion
The greedy algorithm with a min-heap finds the minimum cost to connect all sticks efficiently. It runs in O(n log n) time and uses O(n) auxiliary space, making it more efficient than repeatedly sorting the entire list after every connection.
